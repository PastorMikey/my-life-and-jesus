2. The Start

The Start

Funny thing about life is, once it starts, it just

has an ending point. What happens between the

start and finish has a lot to do with where you go

when you die.

So, in life’s journey, we have this life to live, a

life to die for, a life of self-sacrifice, giving up on

some of the dreams that we had for some of the

dreams that really matter.

God didn’t call us into this world to be self-

serving, but what He wanted and what He asked

for is somebody that would be willing to drag

that cross of self-denial through to the finish.

And in all the attributes of life that we have—the

challenges that we face, the discouragement that

comes our way—we have this hope within us,

brighter than a perfect day, as the song says.

The Start

My life’s coming to an end. There’ll be a lot of

unfinished songs, a lot of unfinished messages,

a lot of things that folks will look back on and

wonder, “What happened to him? Wonder what

really went on in the man’s life.”

I’d like to share that with you today, as we

write it down in this book—not so much of a

biography, but more of a testimony of the great

things that God’s done in my life.

Follow along, if you will. Be patient in the

reading comprehension, what you see and

understand, and allow God to guide your steps,

your path each day, every breath you breathe,

every moment you think,

every thought you have.

Life is a journey. Life is an unexpected turn of

events that turn into great expectations.

3. Childhood & 1st Love

Childhood & 1st Love

I can remember, as a kid, all of the little things

in life that mattered, and it made sense. I didn’t

have no worries of tomorrow. I didn’t have to

worry about work.

If I needed money, I’d just go ask my Mom or

Dad they’d oftentimes direct me to the work that

needed to be done, or chores. Nothing was free,

and we worked for everything we got.

If we wanted anything extra, then again, we’d

have to work for it.

I remember washing everybody’s cars and

trucks, my whole family from Grandparents to

Aunt’s and Uncles or anyone with a car…That was

an opportunity that helped to teach me how to

drive… without a license.

I was a 15 year old boy having fun even if it was

just to back it up a little bit. I feel like I was

accomplishing something.

As I got older, I realized that everything had a

cost, Everything had some type of financial

obligation to be able to do anything.

Childhood & 1st Love

I remember when Daddy first got out of

service and we moved back to Mama’s hometown,

Westlake, Louisiana, in 1975. I began to go to

school in Westlake, Louisiana, at S.P. Arnett, which

was the seventh and eighth grade.

I met a guy named Rodney Dinger who turned

out to be my best friend later in life. And then

something happened. I met his little sister,

“Missy” Melissa was her real name..

Brown and Brown eyes…

Cute as a button, And I fell in love. (possibly 1977)

That was my first real love, that was my Missy

There’s another story to tell behind that as well,

though, ’cause I was dating another girl a few years

later who had actually gone overseas as an

exchange student. This was 1981 I think.

I remember as clear as today, for whatever

reason but I wasn’t saved then, but I really had this

passion for Missy. I really loved her, and I really

felt way down deep in my heart that she would be

my wife.

You know when you got it bad….

Childhood & 1st Love

I can remember, in her mom and daddy’s

living room one night, nobody was around.

I don’t know remember why I was there or if I

stayed the night with Rodney or what it was,

but I remember bowing down on the couch, and I

told God, if He could make my then girlfriend just

kinda go away—not make her disappear, but just go

away, you know—and open the door up that I could

date Missy.

(Granted she was dating another fella, and that

too is another Long story…. )

But back to my Praying to God. I told Him, I

said, “I believe I could marry her and be happy

the rest of my life.”

Not knowing, not knowing the journey that I was

heading down.

Well you guessed it, in 1982, March 3rd Missy

turned 18, in May she Graduated High School

8. We’ll Go…

We’ll Go…

Christmas Day of 1988, I can remember

everybody, as usual, gathered up at Granddaddy’s

house. It just was one of them

times when it seemed like everybody showed up.

Grandpa was sitting in the living room in his

little rocking chair, and he said, “You know, theonly

thing I want for Christmas from anybody in this

house is this one thing: if y’all would just go to

church with me tonight.” And it kind of hit me real

close to my heart, and I said, “I’ll go.” (Because my

wife, as usual, the one that’s got the common sense,

the one that always has the right judgment, and

suggesting for a while we need to go to Church.)

Little do we know what was going to happen

next and the “We’ll go.” Started the biggest family

curry you ever seen or heard in your life. All kinds

of language, all kinds of raising of your voices. Lots

of opinions were cast. And it all hinged around a

statement my Brother Brian made “Well, you

shouldn’t have to go to church just ’cause somebody

asks you to.”

We’ll Go…

So, it’s kind of funny, but at the end of the day,

you gotta realize what was happening. And I just

didn’t understand it THEN. It was just kind of weird.

But we’ll continue and get to the point….

So we went to church that night. The Pastor,

Brother Clarence Campbell, and his wife, Marybeth

Campbell, was the piano player. There was a bunch

of folks there that I didn’t know.

I remember the brother preaching a message,

and I was sitting in the back row, as usual. You

know, newcomers, they sit in the

back. Nowadays we call them backsliders, pews,

’cause a lot of folks get closer to the door before they

leave the church.

But we won’t go down that road. LOL….

I remember when Marybeth got up and was

playing a song, and I think—I think it was “Jesus is

calling.” But I distinctly remember that I just about

couldn’t get up to the altar fast enough.

I just knew that God was dealing with me and

talking to my heart. And all I wanted to do was get

saved.

I was 24, I had a daughter that was 2 years old,

and I had a son that was just born in September. And

I remember cried like a little baby, and my heart was

crying out… then I felt like Jesus sat

next to me saying it’s all good.…

One thing that stands out in my mind today was

when I got back to the pew that I was sitting at, I

took that little baby that was in my arms, and I held

him up to the sky in the church, and I told him, I

said, “Things are going to be diﬀerent from now on.”

I had no idea what I was saying, but I just knew

what I felt.

And from that day, God began to show me the life

that I was living and the life that I needed to live.

Well, they were two diﬀerent lifestyles.

At that little shop that we rented from Miss Dixie

Sparks for about seven years. It didn’t make a whole

lot of money, but God supplied all of our needs.

I can remember a time in that little shop when

me and my wife had done a lot of praying. We were

short on money—short on money to pay bills and the

rent for the shop. This fella walked in, and he asked

us if we was the owner. We told him, “Yes, we was.”

he replied

they are.” And he handed them to me.

what you want to do with it.”

Stuned and excited we thanked him and he left…

13. The Old Tabernacle of Grace

The Old Tabernacle of Grace

Now, there’s a lot of things that happened over

he years while we was at that church in Pineland.

We began to do a lot of traveling, and one of the

places that we frequented was the Vichy Church of

God Campground up by Rolla, Missouri.

There is an old barn there that served as the

main tabernacle, It’s probably about 60 feet wide

and probably 80 feet long or so. An old hay barn

from the 1800s.

That’s where they had church. No air

conditioning, no fans, just an old pole barn, beat-

up pews, bunch of hay all over the place, and a

stage up in the front. The front stage of the church

was probably about 3 feet high.

It was dimly Lit with old hanging lights that few

know how old they were.

At Night a Perfect Missouri breeze blow thru it.

I’ll tell you, if there was ever a place that I felt

the presence of God and I could hear Him talk to

me…. This was it.

If there is any place I grew more spiritually and in

God’s Grace…, it was at that Church campground.

I met a lot of good, God-fearing folks. A lot of

ministers that, through the years, helped me

Inspired me and prayed with me, just allowed me to

grow in grace, They never judged me but sure

challenged my understanding and beliefs and my

Convictions, as we should do each other.

Pray for one another, Love one another and Lift

each other up…

Troubled Times

Now, I can remember a time that I had been

going

through something. My wife and I were going

through a lot of Business Struggles, Life Struggles

and you name it. And it was almost I just couldn’t

hear God somehow.

I just felt like we were overshadowed by doubt

and despair. And the devil was having a heyday in

broke pockets…, and I just could not break through.

If memory serves me right it seemed like this was

going on for six or eight months leading up to this

particular camp meeting,, which is the first weekend

in August every year. It’s been there since the late

1800’s or so..

But there is a dry creek bed out in the woods.

And there’s lots of nice smooth round rocks and

boulders, under the shade of nice trees and no one

around, well I found out there a special spot and I

sat down and begin to talk to God about everything,

I just fell down and I started praying with all my

heart…

Troubled Times

Then I got the mindset that when all the old brothers

in the Bible would fall into trouble, they laid out

stones as places of a memorial, then they would do

what they felt they could do to get a hold of God.

And I began to pray harder than I ever had…

And I began to look into the depths of my heart and

my soul, and I began to examine me. Whatever fault

that I had, whatever I felt was a sin or could lead to

sin, whatever anger and bitterness, lust, or whatever

it was—every time I’d pick up a rock.. Not just any

rock but one the size an shape I thought my problem

was…

I thought about everything I could come up with

that would hinder my prayer, anything against my

brother, whatever it was, I put it down in the creek.

I don’t really know how long I was there, but I

was there long enough that when I got done praying

I felt I had finally reached Heaven. I was crying, and

I was happy. I was shaking with fear, not knowing

exactly what I was supposed to do with the rest of

my life.
